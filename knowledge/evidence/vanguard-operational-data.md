This entry captures the operational, demographic, and sizing figures cleanly so we can build the business case ([[../strategy/respite-marketplace-concept]]) on top of a known substrate.

## The whiteboard — demand extrapolation

The primary sizing artifact from the meeting. Vanguard's Jason wrote this on the whiteboard when Shobhit pushed for a "how big is this?" number:

```
Seniors                    1,000,000
  AAC:      70% robust        700K
  non-robust: 30%             300K

Current attach rate/month
  in Pasir Ris:              5–10%

est. # of bookings/month:    15–30K nationwide
```

Reading this out:

- **~1 million seniors aged 60+** in Singapore.
- Of these, **~70% are robust** (well, active, generally healthy — the target population for [[active-ageing-centres]]).
- **~30% are non-robust** (~300K) — needing some level of care support, spanning frail through severely frail.
- Vanguard's Pasir Ris operations see a **5–10% monthly attach rate for respite-adjacent services** among active caregiver clients.
- Extrapolated to the national base: **15,000–30,000 chaperone / respite bookings per month.**

The extrapolation carries assumptions we should be honest about:

- Pasir Ris may not be representative (it's not the oldest sub-region, but not the youngest either — Ang Mo Kio and Toa Payoh are older).
- The 5–10% attach rate is measured against Vanguard's *active client base*, not the total 15,000 senior population of the sub-region.
- Vanguard's supply constraints suppress observed demand — they turn down 3–4 in 20 requests. Actual demand ceiling could be much higher.

Verified by [[aic]]-held data would tighten this number substantially — see the AIC entry for the follow-up meeting we need to book.

## Sub-region data

- **Pasir Ris:** ~15,000 seniors in one of the two Pasir Ris sub-regions Vanguard operates.
- Sub-regions are based on URA planning zones. 85 total nationally under [[iccp-model]].
- Not extreme demographically — Vanguard used Pasir Ris as a "representative" example.
- **Ang Mo Kio, Toa Payoh** — much older demographic profiles. Harder sub-regions.
- Older estates are where the demand is likely most concentrated but also where care infrastructure is most stretched.

## Population health data (via PHV, administered by AIC)

The Population Health Visit is a ~3-page in-home survey administered by [[aic]] using the *SGO* framework (single-generation-of-something, name partially garbled in the transcript). Covers whether the senior has help, whether they feel they need support, how they're coping.

**Loneliness and social isolation:**

- **Less than 3%** of seniors report feeling isolated or lonely.
- Two distinct measures tracked separately:
  - Low or no social interactions (objective)
  - Subjective feeling of loneliness
- The two don't always correlate. A senior may live with family and still feel lonely.
- Seniors living alone are considered more at risk.

**Frailty and ADL needs:**

- **~19% of seniors** have basic ADL (Activities of Daily Living) needs — the frailer segment.
- **20–30%** fall in a middle tier depending on sub-region and socioeconomic profile.
- **60–70%** well and robust nationally.

**Note on the smaller numbers:** Lara's AI query asked about *"1.8% seniors feeling lonely, 2.6% limited social interactions, 7% frail, 3% severely frail"* — those specific figures were not captured in the transcript. They may be from a Vanguard slide not shared, or from an aggregate document. Worth asking Jason to send.

## Chaperone service — operational scale

- **160 clients served daily** across 4 senior-care centres.
- **20–30 chaperone appointments per month** — not per centre, across the four.
- **Rejection rate: 3–4 out of every 20** requests turned away, capacity-limited.
- **Request lead time: 3 days advance notice** typical.

## Chaperone service — pricing

| Party | Rate |
|---|---|
| Micro-worker pay | $10–12/hour |
| Market rate for comparable gig | $20–22/hour |
| Vanguard client charge | $25–35/hour |
| Cost basis per session (fully loaded) | ~$26 |
| Subsidised senior-care-centre client rate | as low as $5/day |

Willingness-to-pay is clearly below the un-subsidised price for many clients. Vanguard uses an internal healthcare fund plus philanthropic capital to bridge. This is where the multi-payer stack argument in [[../strategy/respite-marketplace-concept]] comes from.

## Vanguard supply capacity

- **3–4 trained Vanguard staff per senior-care centre** (salaried, mainstream service track).
- **19–20 senior micro-jobbers per centre** — well/robust seniors in their early 60s, early retirees.
- **~76–80 micro-workers total** across the four centres if we compute it out.
- **First formal micro-worker onboarding cycle starts July 2026.** Currently pilot scale.

## AAC national footprint

- **~240 Active Ageing Centres** across Singapore.
- One AAC serves **1,500–4,000 seniors** in its catchment (~3,000 as working figure).
- **Only 15–25% of seniors** in a catchment area are actually reached by their local AAC — capacity constraints plus the get-them-to-come-out problem.
- The **15–25% who show up are not the ones most at risk**; the concern is about the unreached majority.

## Vanguard nursing home capacity

- **5 nursing homes, ~1,200 beds.**
- Plan to **double capacity in 5–7 years.**
- Assisted living pilot at Vivado; next site Bedok. HDB/MND collaboration.
- 3 new senior-care/active-ageing centres opening next year in the Marsiling Ridge area.

## Training programme details

Micro-worker training tracks (fully in-house, free to trainees):

- **CPR (VCRF EAD)** — externally certified, at St. Luke's Hospital. Mandatory.
- **Community Assistance and Conserve Bandwidth** — externally certified modules.
- **Mobility assistance** — half-day in-house.
- **Dementia basics** — in-house tiered module; not scientifically rigorous per Vanguard's own admission, but covers typical behaviours and interaction techniques.
- **Emergency management, SOPs, OJT** — in-house.

Regulatory-track training (mainstream, ministry-funded) has strict competency requirements including professional nursing-board certifications for clinical staff.
