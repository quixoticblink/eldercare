ok so on 20 July the cohort masterclass ran a speed-carousel: groups rotated past each other's boards, left post-its, and talked. This is the first time the reframed HMW, the Kakis concept, and the prototype story met an audience outside our partner chain — and the feedback splits cleanly into *warm on the thinking, sharp on the unanswered commercials, and one genuinely uncomfortable methodological critique* from a facilitator who had seen many groups.

**Evidence caveat.** The board photo covers our section (labelled Group 9); the audio is a rough auto-transcription of ~6 minutes of carousel conversation — speaker attribution is inferred, wording is approximate, and several names were garbled ("Wagner" = Vanguard, "hotmail" = HMW). Quotes below are cleaned for sense, not verbatim-reliable. Treat as directional.

## What landed

Three post-its and the room agreed on the same things: the research-first framing ("good focus on research to frame the problem well"), the reframe itself ("I like the reframe of your problem statement"), and **micro-credentials as a real differentiator**. The consistency thesis also resonated unprompted — one visitor articulated our own design back at us: *"you only have to deal with [vetting] a micro-caregiver once, and as a caregiver you feel this person gets along with my elderly, so can I get the same person again and again… I would definitely [use] this service I can trust."*

## The sharpest critique — "give it to caregivers, don't sell it to Vanguard"

A facilitator who had reviewed many groups leaned in with the one piece of feedback worth restructuring around: *"When you are building a prototype — try and be less salesy. Your prototype must go not to Vanguard, but to the caregivers… too much money is spent building marketplaces and training people that nobody uses. Figure out the two or three things you will build for, give it to the caregiver — not selling to them and convincing them, but giving to them. Otherwise it becomes an echo chamber: I got this feature, that feature."*

That lands squarely on a bias the wiki already half-knows: since June, every validation conversation has been with the *supply and partnership* side (NCSS, Vanguard, AIC-adjacent). Round-1 caregiver interviews predate the concept. **Nobody who would actually book a Kakis visit has touched the prototype.** The post-it version of the same point: "top 2–3 things to test!!" — and it should gate the Aug 20 milestone: pick the two or three caregiver-facing moments (likely: crisis activation speed, the known-face match, the price after subsidy) and put the prototype in caregivers' hands before building anything else.

## The four pressure clusters

**Differentiation.** "How is this different from Homage?" · "What were other solutions considered & rejected?" · "How will you compete in a crowded marketplace?" — reviewers named Homage, Luce, and **Kampung Kakis** unprompted. Two takeaways: the competitive-landscape answer exists in the wiki ([[../landscape/luce-sg]], the rejected-alternatives record in [[../reframing/hmw-evolution]]) but isn't in the pitch — a presentation gap, not a thinking gap. And the Kampung Kakis mention doubles as a **name-collision warning**: an established ground-up befriending initiative already owns "kakis" in this space; our provisional name likely can't survive as-is.

**Safeguarding and abuse prevention.** "How do you qualify respite givers to prevent abuse?" — pushed further by the Group 9 note that *some elderly can't speak up against abuse*, with live surveillance floated ("could be a body[cam]… recording for evaluation — it can go both ways"). The room's own counterweight: trust cuts both ways, and cameras in homes collide with dignity and privacy. Our current answers (tiered certification, staff-paired first visits, OTP-verified visits, the quality agent reading every report both ways) are necessary but the question deserves a first-class written answer — including why we would *not* adopt surveillance, and what we'd do instead for seniors who can't self-report.

**Commercials.** "How will the pricing be decided?" · "Any accreditation fees for respite givers?" — both currently [ops] questions with Vanguard (G2/G3 in the spec), now asked externally too. On vetting cost-bearing, one visitor argued the platform *shouldn't* absorb full vetting responsibility — push some to reviews and family interviews, "then trust slowly builds." Which re-raises the MOH constraint: we told the room MOH explicitly disallows rating of caregivers and asked for implicit-review ideas; the honest answer we got was "start cautiously."

**Scope.** "Is this for elderly only, or anyone incapacitated?" — babysitting and general respite came up. The room's own advice matched ours: *start narrow* with the elderly wedge. Also methodological: quantify demand — how many visits, which services demanded that operators can't fulfil ("they cannot fulfil a lot of services," per what Vanguard told us — the AIC data ask again). Plus two smaller seeds: cluster providers against needs (e.g., exercise companions), and a **Smart City tie-in — one visitor offered to work with it "under the smart cities network,"** a follow-up worth chasing by name.

## What this changes

- **Aug 20 milestone re-aimed:** the prototype's next test audience is caregivers, not partners. Define the 2–3 caregiver-facing moments to test; a video walkthrough given (not pitched) to the caregiver pool is acceptable form.
- **Pitch gains a "considered and rejected" beat** — the material exists in `reframing/`; surface it.
- **Safeguarding needs its own entry** — [[safeguarding-and-abuse-prevention]] (proposed): certification tiers, paired first visits, OTP presence-proof, both-ways quality reading, and the reasoned case against in-home surveillance.
- **Name decision accelerated** — Kampung Kakis collision logged; the naming question from [[../strategy/respite-marketplace-concept]] is no longer a day-2 problem.
- **New follow-up thread:** the Smart Cities Network offer from the carousel.
