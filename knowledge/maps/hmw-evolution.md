Five iterations, ~seven weeks. Each one narrower than the last — until the fifth, which deliberately widens the fourth back out. Not a straight line: an inheritance chain where each iteration keeps what still works and drops what field evidence broke, and one place where we corrected our own overshoot three days later. This map shows what changed at each step and why.

## Iteration 0 — 2026-05-19 · [[../reframing/hmw-current]] (May 19 synthesis)

> How might we help elderly in Singapore experience genuine daily connection, so that Singapore is brimming with elderly communities that are happy, mentally and physically healthy?

**WHO:** elderly in Singapore (general).
**DO:** experience genuine daily connection.
**OUTCOME:** Singapore brimming with elderly communities that are happy, mentally and physically healthy.

**Origin:** synthesis of six individual WHO/DO/OUTCOME rows written by five team members ([[../reframing/hmw-2026-05-19-individual-exercise]]). DO phrasing carried forward jointly from Zheng Wei's row + Shobhit's row; OUTCOME synthesised by Shobhit combining his own "quality of aging" language with Lara's "brimming" phrase. WHO deliberately left broad.

**Strength:** family stories from all five team members pointed at the same word — connection. The frame that all-five-of-us-touch matters emotionally at pitch time.

**Weakness (recognised later):** "elderly in Singapore" is ~1.1M people who don't have one shape. "Genuine daily connection" is broad enough to admit almost any intervention, including many that don't work.

## Iteration 1 — 2026-06-15 · [[../reframing/hmw-2026-06-15-ncss-reframe-signal]] (reframe signal, not a full iteration)

Not a new HMW. A placeholder — recording the tension from the NCSS meeting without acting on it. NCSS presented their own year-old HMW:

> How might we enable caregivers to conveniently access respite in a timely manner?

Aditi conceded on-camera: *"Your problem statement is a lot more specific than ours."* The signal file laid out three options:
1. Adopt NCSS's HMW verbatim.
2. Nest ours inside theirs.
3. Reframe from scratch.

**What triggered it:** NCSS's IDIs shared at the 2026-06-15 meeting, especially the "consistency" finding (seniors *repel* new helpers, need months to adjust). This finding partially invalidated the on-demand shape of any solution ours was pointing at.

**What it delayed:** the actual reframe. The team wanted to sit with the tension before committing.

## Iteration 2 — 2026-06-25 · [[../reframing/hmw-2026-06-25-caregiver-respite]] (caregiver respite reframe)

> How might we offer on-demand respite to caregivers of the relatively healthy elderly in Singapore?

**What moved:**
- **WHO** — from "elderly" to "caregivers of elderly." A different user, a different pain-point-holder, a different buyer.
- **DO** — from "genuine daily connection" to "on-demand respite." Concrete, buildable.
- **WHO further narrowed** — "relatively healthy elderly" removes the frail/bedridden (they need clinical care) and the fully independent (they don't need caregivers).

**What stayed:**
- Elderly still the ultimate beneficiary.
- Aditi's healthcare-burden reframe as the strategic outcome.
- Consistency still the design constraint.
- Companionship absorbed as one respite category, not lost.

**What triggered it:** the June 25 solutioning session ([[../journal/2026-06-25-solutioning]]). Aditi's Padlet formulation ("HMW offer on-demand respite to caregivers for the relatively healthy elderly") became the team's working draft. Abhishek's [[../strategy/marketplace-2x2]] framework put the marketplace positioning debate on rails.

## Iteration 3 — 2026-07-08 · [[../reframing/hmw-2026-07-08-post-vanguard]]

> How might we enable caregivers of the relatively healthy elderly in Singapore to activate trusted respite when a crisis hits — a hospitalisation, a helper runaway, a spouse in decline — layered onto Vanguard's Pasir Ris ICCP pilot as our first operational proof.

**What moved:**
- **DO** — from "on-demand respite" to "activate trusted respite when a crisis hits." *Activate* implies threshold-triggered, not discretionary. Closer to insurance than to Uber. Trust is now on the front label.
- **WHERE named** — "Vanguard's Pasir Ris ICCP pilot." No longer a national marketplace in the abstract; a specific piece of a specific pilot on a specific existing platform.
- **Six crisis triggers named explicitly** — helper runaway, spousal-couple emergency, discharge scramble, sudden decline, grief, caregiver's own emergency.

**What stayed:**
- Same WHO (caregivers, relatively healthy elderly).
- Same OUTCOME logic (healthcare burden, sandwich generation).
- Consistency constraint still binding.
- Companionship still one category among six.

**What triggered it:** the Vanguard site visit ([[../journal/2026-07-08-vanguard-visit]]). Two moves in the meeting: (1) Shobhit's "step-back" forced Vanguard to name what the *real* pain is (Angeline named the six triggers), and (2) Angeline volunteered a Pasir Ris pilot by year-end.

**What's still weak:** ownership after September is still unresolved. Consistency-vs-on-demand tension has an operational hint (Vanguard's phone-triage pattern, [[../landscape/community-chaperone]]) but not a scaled answer.

## Iteration 4 — 2026-07-11 · [[../reframing/hmw-2026-07-11-broader-need]] (current working HMW)

> How might we enable caregivers of the relatively healthy elderly in Singapore to activate trusted respite when a need arises — planned or urgent — layered onto Vanguard's Pasir Ris ICCP pilot as our first operational proof.

The only iteration that *widens* rather than narrows, and the only one triggered by us rather than by a partner.

**What moved:**
- **DO** — from "when a crisis hits" to "when a need arises — planned or urgent." Planned respite (an office meeting overlapping a medical appointment, a vacation needing daily check-ins, a wedding or work travel) is the same service with a longer lead time.

**What stayed:** everything else. Same WHO, same WHERE (Pasir Ris ICCP), same WHEN (year-end 2026), same six crisis triggers — they're now the urgent half of the demand curve rather than the whole of it.

**What triggered it:** us, three days later, with no new external evidence. The Jul 8 sentence was faithful to what Vanguard named as their sharpest pain, and we mistook *sharpest* for *whole*. Crisis-only is where willingness-to-pay is clearest but volume is thinnest — a product that occupies mind-share on the day it's needed and then disappears for eleven months is hard to remember, staff, or economically justify.

**What's still weak:** two demand curves now live in one product. Planned, low-urgency respite and urgent, high-stakes activation may want different UX, different SLAs, different prices. Nothing has tested that they can share a surface.

**Worth noting for the rubric:** this is the clearest single piece of evidence for the "learning and adaptation" line — a correction we made against our own three-day-old work, and against the framing of the partner we most wanted to please.

## What ties the iterations together

Three threads run through all five:

**Consistency as the sharpest constraint.** Named quietly at May 19 (Zheng Wei's "80% AI, 20% human" hedge), sharpened at June 15 by NCSS's IDIs, canonised at July 8 in Vanguard's operational pattern. Now written up as [[../strategy/consistency-as-design-constraint]].

**The multi-payer stack.** Named as the funding-model implication at June 25, sharpened at July 8 when Vanguard's own pricing tension ($10-12 pay vs $25-35 charge, subsidised through internal healthcare fund + philanthropic capital) proved the pattern. Referenced in [[../strategy/respite-marketplace-concept]] but doesn't have its own entry yet — one of the highest-value [[proposed-stubs]].

**The moat is not code.** May 19 didn't even think about a moat. June 25 said "the algorithm isn't proprietary." July 8 confirmed: certification, trust, and ecosystem density are the moat, none of which is software. This softens [[../reframing/devils-advocate]] critique 8 into an operational specification rather than a debate.

## The evidence that moved us at each step

- May 19 → June 15: our own round 1 field interviews ([[../evidence/interview-round-1-findings]]) plus NCSS's IDIs ([[../evidence/ncss-idi-findings]]).
- June 15 → June 25: sitting with the reframe signal for ten days; the marketplace 2×2 debate on June 25.
- June 25 → July 8: Vanguard's operational reality ([[../evidence/vanguard-operational-data]]) and the six crisis triggers named in the meeting.
- July 8 → July 11: no new evidence at all — a team re-reading of our own sentence. The only iteration produced by argument rather than fieldwork.

## What's next

The next iteration — likely after AIC ([[../landscape/aic]]) and Care Corner ([[../landscape/care-corner]]) meetings — should:
- **Sharpen the multi-payer stack** with actual dollars and payer boundaries.
- **Resolve the ownership question** — HCLI Tech as counterparty vs NGO-of-our-own vs commercial spin-off.
- **Name the AI companion's role** more precisely — the blue-sky data-labeling → humanoid pipeline is on the roadmap; the near-term product is not AI-first.

## Devil's advocate carried across iterations

The critique list at [[../reframing/devils-advocate]] has grown from five (May 19) to fourteen (July 8), and to fifteen after the Jul 20 masterclass — critique 15 being the facilitator's *"your prototype must go to the caregivers, not Vanguard."* Critiques 11 (ownership) and 12 (partner over-optimism) have partially softened after Vanguard. Critiques 6 (AI half-life), 8 (algorithm not the moat), 13 (marketplace framing doesn't fit home care), and 14 (MOH won't allow preference-matching UX) are now design constraints rather than open questions. Critiques 7 (deck same as one year ago) and 10 (latent demand, awareness gap) remain active.
