This is the *evening* companion to [[2026-05-25]] — same date, different event. The interview synthesis is captured there. This entry is the working session where the team consolidated everything into what became the May 26 cohort presentation.

## The arc of the session

Shobhit opened with the submission structure: everything centred on the microsite Abhishek had built, sections for problem context, discovery, empathy map, and journey. Aditi pushed back on trimming — we still need enough problem-space setup for the HMW to land. Group aligned: the microsite is the single artifact, and we build 4 quick slides for the live 5-minute presentation on top of it.

Shobhit then walked through an AI-synthesized empathy map he'd built from the round 1 interview responses. Themes: preference for family care, invisibility and abandonment, transient tech, being *needed* rather than *cared for*. Zheng Wei interrupted with a methodological concern — we never actually asked interviewees whether they were lonely. He argued we need a "loneliness marker," at least a behavioural one. That opened a real conversation.

Zheng Wei's contribution here was probably the most conceptually generative part of the evening. He offered a two-spectrum framing: lonely elderly tend to sit at one of two extremes — strong-personality-push-away (proud, uncompromising) or dependent-collapse (over-relied on a person who then went away). His unempathetic-but-honest phrasing: *"loneliness is some kind of a choice."* Meaning: the elderly who show up at the AAC are self-selecting; the people we should worry about are the ones who don't.

He also floated the AI-plus-human blend for the first time — *"80% AI, 20% real human."* Seed of what became a live debate weeks later.

The middle chunk (roughly an hour) was a live git working session. Abhishek walked the team through the repo structure (knowledge/ + site/), Vercel auto-deploy, and codified the two-step workflow: **update knowledge → run the microsite prompt → push both.** Collaborators added, SSH keys fumbled, PRs approved, branch protection discussed. Aditi acted as second-in-command on the git flow, translating for the rest of us. The empathy map got committed. The site build broke; Abhishek said he'd fix it offline.

Final thirty minutes were presentation choreography.

## What we learned

- The team's own framing of the problem deepened out loud. From "loneliness" as a monolith to *"complex, multi-threaded problem"* — Aditi's phrase, which became the closing beat of the stats section.
- Zheng Wei's two-spectrum framing changed how we think about *reach*. Activity-based interventions only touch the people who already come out; the pride-gated and the collapsed-after-loss subsets are the ones the AAC-model misses.
- Shobhit's Toa Payoh interviewee delivered the sharpest reframe of what loneliness actually is: *"his biggest fear is not talking to anyone the whole day, while he lives with his wife."* Loneliness is about being unseen, not being alone. That line survives into the empathy map.
- Aditi noted that her own interviewee "would not strictly classify as lonely" — the round 1 sample skewed toward the non-lonely. Honest limitation, not fatal, but worth flagging when we quote the round 1 findings.

## What changed

- **HMW held constant** — Aditi's explicit call: *"let's not change that, because it needs to be broad."* This was a real decision, not drift.
- **The microsite is now the working artifact**, not a submission add-on. Everything else (slides, transcripts, evidence) points at it.
- **The knowledge → prompt → push workflow codified**. Abhishek to be the sole runner of the site-generation prompt going forward. Everyone else focuses on the knowledge base. This is the pattern that's held since.
- **A methodological gap acknowledged**: we did not test for loneliness directly in round 1. We know it now.
