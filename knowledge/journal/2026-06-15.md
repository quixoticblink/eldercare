Roughly four weeks in. We met the **National Council of Social Service** (NCSS, [ncss.gov.sg](https://www.ncss.gov.sg)) — the statutory body that funds and coordinates most of Singapore's ~150 Social Service Agencies. They convened the meeting because they'd been running their own ideation on eldercare through the Social Changemaker Series, and had heard from SGLN that we were working the same space.

The short version: NCSS came in with a **sharper HMW than ours** — they've had it for over a year — and by the end of the call Aditi said out loud what several of us were thinking, that their framing is more focused than ours and we should probably lean into it. This is the first genuine reframe signal since May 19. Recorded but not resolved: see [[../reframing/hmw-2026-06-15-ncss-reframe-signal]].

## The arc of the meeting

**NCSS opened with framing.** They described prior work on social-impact metrics and pitched the thesis they're building around: *"leveraging corporate assets to solve complex social issues,"* with Singapore as a launchpad for regional scaling backed by philanthropic and family-office capital.

**We introduced ourselves.** Five members, six-month sprint, MVP required by September ("not just slides, something which is working"), currently ideating around companionship/loneliness among the elderly but open to broader problem statements.

**NCSS walked through their research journey.** Secondary research → validation with 2–3 SSAs (Caring SG, CAL / Caregiver Alliance, Care Corner) → the *Social Changemaker Series* last July (corporates + government + MSF + SSAs, split into caregiver / youth / senior tracks) → IDIs with seniors and caregivers via Brett + Care Corner + SMU in late 2025 (~20 minutes each, Chinese/Malay/English, ~20–30 respondents). Their HMW landed on:

> **How might we enable caregivers to conveniently access respite in a timely manner?**

Note the pivot inside their own framing — the DO is not *give respite to the caregiver,* it's *give access to respite* to the caregiver. That's a subtle but load-bearing distinction.

**They named a white space concretely.** Existing market services — HomeAge, Jagami — take *2–3 weeks* to confirm an appointment. *Over 50%* of potential beneficiaries don't know these services exist. Neither existing provider handles the sudden-and-unexpected case: grandmother sick, caregiver has a meeting, someone trusted needs to accompany her to the clinic *today.* That's the wedge.

**Aditi and Shobhit pushed back on the diagnosis.** Aditi called the summary insights "obvious/superficial." Shobhit repeatedly pressed: *what is the actual need* — language, tech literacy, access, trust? He got NCSS to articulate the on-demand-escort scenario as the concrete case, not respite in the abstract.

**Aditi brought marketplace references.** UrbanClap, Fiverr, Snabbit (India, hyperlocal home-help). The group debated centralised/certified (Grab-like) vs open (Shopee-like) and agreed for an eldercare MVP it has to be **controlled and certified** — trust is the constraint, not selection.

**NCSS revealed Vanguard.** After their corporate outreach to Grab and Gojek both failed (Gojek said no after one round; Grab found no business case), NCSS pivoted to **Vanguard** — a unit under MOH Holdings running Active Ageing Centres and testbed pilots. Vanguard is already piloting **Community Chaperone** (on-demand escort) and **Night Respite** (one staff on standby). Both are "well-received" but there is no formal outcome survey. Manual matching. Supply-side bottleneck — staffing. NCSS has AIC's (Agency for Integrated Care) blessing to work with Vanguard.

**We pushed on the missing measurement.** No pilot survey. NCSS extrapolates outcomes from demographic profiles. This is a real gap. Any business case we build for them will be light on outcome data by default.

**The reframe moment.** Aditi said it plainly: *"Your problem statement is a lot more specific than ours, to be very honest. Ours is quite broad at this point… companionship, or reducing loneliness among elderly. This is a very, very big problem statement, while I think yours is a lot more focused."* NCSS in turn suggested our companionship angle could sit *inside* the marketplace ("that could be one part of the marketplace"). Neither side was pushing for a merger; both signalled willingness. See the reframe-signal file for the full analysis.

**Ownership tension surfaced.** NCSS was explicit: *"Definitely not at NCSS… we need to get someone to own it."* Aditi drew a line: *"I would not spend our effort over the next three months building something that nobody would take it forward."* This is the biggest strategic risk on our horizon.

**Strategy-vs-product tangent.** NCSS asked how business cases work inside tech companies. We explained single-threaded leadership, quantifiable impact, extrapolation from demographic profile. The fintech-transformed-banking analogy came from NCSS.

**Close.** Meet Vanguard soon (Woodlands centre; school holidays are a scheduling risk). Run more caregiver IDIs via Vanguard's pool. Refine the merged problem statement. Build the business case + POC. Selfie for documentation.

## What we learned

- NCSS has a **sharper HMW than we do** and has held it for a year. Our companionship framing is broader; theirs is more focused. Aditi conceded the point.
- The **consistency insight from NCSS's IDIs contradicts a design instinct** we hadn't fully examined: seniors don't want a fresh helper every day — they need *months* to adjust to someone new. This complicates on-demand ("stranger arrives, senior repels") in a way we hadn't priced in. See [[../evidence/ncss-idi-findings]].
- The Grab/Gojek failure was on **business case grounds, not tech grounds** — the matching algorithm is not the moat. What is the moat? Certification, trust, and ecosystem density — not code.
- Vanguard is running two concrete pilots (**Community Chaperone**, **Night Respite**) that we can *see* — but neither has a formal outcome survey. "Well-received" is anecdotal.
- The Singapore **awareness gap is real**: more than half of potential beneficiaries don't know existing services exist. Demand is latent, not observable.
- NCSS's **ecosystem thesis** — paid caregivers plus volunteers plus bereaved former caregivers plus workers displaced by AI/automation — is the supply-side story they want us to help build.

## What changed

- We are leaning toward **caregiver respite as the wedge**, with companionship nested as one respite category — but not committed yet. See [[../reframing/hmw-2026-06-15-ncss-reframe-signal]].
- Vanguard is now a live thread — potential anchoring organisation and IDI source.
- The **AI-companion path is materially less defensible** than it was a week ago. NCSS confirmed what the round 1 solution provider told us: patterns get detected, users disengage. Abhishek conceded on the call: *"the intelligence is available now, the challenge is acceptance."*
- We have a real external ask: build a business case with quantifiable impact numbers (NCSS's example: "we're gonna impact 100,000 seniors"). This gives the pitch a target, not just a story.
