Third partner session, and the first one where the question was no longer *is this
worth building* but *how do we put it in front of people without hurting anyone*.
The answer that came back was smaller than we wanted and better than we asked for:
don't launch, run a **tabletop exercise with a limited user set** first.

## What changed

- The August milestone stops being "prototype in caregivers' hands" and becomes
  **a tabletop with a small, named group** — walk the journeys with real people,
  in a room, before anyone's mother is on the other end of a booking.
- A limited user set means we can hand-verify every account, which is exactly what
  the app currently requires anyway: SNS is still sandboxed for `ap-southeast-1`,
  so SMS reaches only numbers we register. Constraint and plan happen to agree.
- The pilot design conversation moved from *what would we build* to *what would we
  measure* — which is the conversation the masterclass facilitator told us on
  Jul 20 we were avoiding. See [[2026-07-20-masterclass]], critique 15.

## Why a tabletop is the right smaller thing

The thing that took me a while to see: our whole validation record is supply-side.
Vanguard, NCSS, the cohort — all people who assess the *idea*. Not one caregiver has
touched the product. A tabletop is the cheapest instrument that changes that without
putting a vulnerable senior in the loop on day one. Nobody gets hurt by a walkthrough.

It also front-loads the failure we can least afford. If a caregiver can't find the
start code, or a kaki can't work out how to accept, we find out across a table rather
than at someone's front door at 9pm.
