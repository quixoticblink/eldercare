Third partner session. The first two were about whether the idea was worth building; this
one was about putting it in front of people, and it went somewhere we didn't plan. We came
asking for a live pilot before the 25 September presentation. Vanguard said no — and then
offered something better than a polite no.

## The counter-proposal

> *"if it's really looking at testing whether this work to reduce the [admin] type of work,
> I think maybe we're really rolling it out more of a tabletop exercise model that we have a
> few of seniors and staff sitting in and trying out the app like a UAT. Try all the
> workflow… The 2 or 3 groups in a very safe environment, rather than directly rolling it
> out already and having all the hiccups and all the issues that we have to address and
> resolve later on."* — Vanguard

We took it almost immediately, and not as a concession. The reason we took it is the useful
part:

> *"us wanting to get real feedback on whether this is going to be helpful or not. From our
> point of view, this seems sufficient-ish, but then from their point of view, like the
> tabletop exercise thing, they might have more ideas or resistance or whatever else."* — SGLN

We want the resistance. Seven weeks of partner meetings have produced agreement, and
agreement is not evidence.

**Shape of it:** roughly five sets (staff + micro-jobber + senior), run as two or three
groups, one afternoon, ~2 hours from 2pm with refreshments. **Two separate sessions** — one
Vanguard arranges, one NCSS/Care Corner arranges. Target the week of **17 August**, after
the long weekend. Co-facilitated, with a deliberate anti-anchoring rule: let people play
before we explain. Three roles have to be in the room — caregiver or elderly, micro-jobber,
and the admin in the middle — which maps exactly onto the three modules we built.

Seniors are to be recruited from **prior service users**, chosen for tolerance:
*"We want to have people who are more receptive to something that may go wrong."* That has a
methodological cost worth naming — the friendliest possible users are the least likely to
show us cold-start friction.

## The flattest thing said all meeting

> *"to see impact, we definitely need more than seven weeks. And because while this is a good
> project, I mean, the intention outcome is good, but we do have already existing other work
> in our pipeline."* — Vanguard

And the honest version of what a September deadline can actually buy:

> *"It may not be feasible for us to run an actual pilot to give you enough stats for the
> presentation… But if you just want a very simple UAT, you know, just try out the user
> interface. Then I can give you."*

## Supply is the binding constraint, not demand

This inverts the usual marketplace assumption and it reframes the whole product.

> *"it's pretty resource intensive, and the demand is not exactly that great yet. It's
> actually an [chicken-and-]egg lah. So we don't have to buy this size, because we are worried
> that should the demand be so high, we don't have the supply."* — Vanguard

> *"Once you push this out, even if it's a pilot, I need to manage the caregivers and the
> seniors expectations. You may expect that… wow, I'm book ready. I want it immediately. But
> I… may not have that number of micro job or staff."* — Vanguard

That second one is the closest the meeting comes to a duty-of-care argument, and it's the
right one: a booking promise the operator can't honour is a harm to a vulnerable person, not
a bad user experience.

Certification gates supply hard — mobility training → on-the-job training → activation, with
the next CPR cohort a month or more out, and OJT needing a live case that may not exist. Our
workaround, accepted in principle: **whitelist who's already trained** rather than wait.
*"of these 20 microjobbers available, these only three can offer CPR because they already
have that training. And the rest will just upload their cert whenever they do."* Those are
the only concrete supply numbers anywhere in the document.

## The thing nobody said out loud

Vanguard reframed the success metric: *"See whether it has helped to lessen your
administrative burden and how user friendly it is."*

That is an **operator-efficiency metric**. It quietly moves the primary user from the
caregiver to Vanguard's coordinator — which is a different product from the one the HMW
describes. Set it next to NCSS pushing back on the micro-jobs framing (*"Shouldn't be
[micro-jobs] more from giving a more resilient caregiving… bear in mind is the ultimate goal"*)
and we now have **three framings live at once**: caregiver relief, resilient caregiving, and
operational digitisation of an existing manual service. Nobody reconciled them. That is the
most important unresolved thing in the meeting and it isn't on anyone's action list.

## What changed

- **Launch → tabletop.** No operational pilot inside the seven-week window. Live matching
  continues on a parallel track, decoupled from 25 September.
- **Scope narrows to one service — chaperoning.** Warmly received, not hard-committed. The
  rest of the service menu becomes roadmap.
- **Delivery leans web-first.** *"So there's only one download"* — a direct answer to "don't
  make seniors install another app."
- **Certification tracking becomes app work**, committed by us on the call.
- **Standalone vs embedded stays open.** Vanguard wants it inside the AIC senior app; NCSS
  isn't sure; our position is staged — outside for the pilot, integrated after.
- **Restated, unchanged:** no ratings. Payments stay cash/manual for the pilot; PayNow
  deferred. Chinese localisation confirmed feasible.

## What's next

- **Fix the app login access issue** so participants reach the live app, not just the
