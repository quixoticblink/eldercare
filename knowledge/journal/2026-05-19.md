First working session of the sprint. All five of us on Zoom.

## The arc of the day

We didn't walk in knowing what the problem was. The day went, roughly:

1. **Brainstorming.** Open-ended — what problems do we care about, what's happening in our own lives, what does each of us know that the others don't. Everyone threw things on the wall.
2. **Desktop research.** Each of us pulled in stats, news, founder stories, and prior art from our networks. Aging demographics, elderly loneliness surveys, existing initiatives in Singapore and elsewhere.
3. **Sharing multiple ideas.** We surfaced roughly 11 distinct angles — reminiscence therapy (Memory Lane), caregiver support (Oak Care), digital inclusion, Grab's voice-accessibility product, the NCSS GrabCare postmortem, elderly mobility and safety, etc. (See [[../landscape/README]] — these are the seeds for the landscape folder.)
4. **Narrowing on eldercare companionship.** As we compared the ideas against our own family situations — Abhishek's parents in India, Aditi's view from a family that lost a life partner, Lara's recently-relocated elderly parent, Shobhit's in-laws from Bangkok, Zheng Wei's clinical lens — companionship/loneliness came up as the unifying word. Care had options. Connection didn't.
5. **How Might We exercise.** Each of us wrote our own HMW using the WHO / DO / TO scaffold. Six attempts in total (Zheng Wei wrote two). Verbatim record: [[hmw-2026-05-19-individual-exercise]].
6. **Narrowing down to one HMW.** We synthesized the six into a single sentence: *How might we help elderly in Singapore experience genuine daily connection, so that Singapore is brimming with elderly communities that are happy, mentally and physically healthy?* See [[hmw-current]] for the full synthesis and reasoning.

## What we learned

- Even before talking to elderly people, our own family stories already pointed to "connection" as the unifying word, not "care" or "support." Five different family situations, same underlying pattern.
- The team had naturally narrowed the WHO in different ways (couple, no-kids, isolated, lonely, comprehensive). We chose to keep the WHO general in the HMW and let empathy work narrow it later.
- The DO that won — "experience genuine daily connection" — beat softer options because it names both quality (*genuine*) and rhythm (*daily*) in one phrase.
- The TO that won — "Singapore brimming with elderly communities..." — pulled the outcome from individual to societal scale. The others were individual-shaped.
