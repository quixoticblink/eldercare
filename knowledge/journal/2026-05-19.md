First working session of the sprint. Rewritten on 2026-05-25 from the meeting transcript after realising the earlier draft was based on second-hand inference.

## The arc of the day

The session opened with a round-the-table of prior inspirations, not a formal brainstorm. Lara anchored with two real-world case studies — Memory Lane (reminiscence therapy) and Oak Care (caregiver empowerment) — as example problem statements to react to. Zheng Wei brought his friend's HCAH startup (India, elderly healthcare across Southeast Asia) and its three pain buckets: staff training / quality of care, "the epidemic of loneliness," and physical activeness in old age. Aditi introduced digital exclusion via HDB Singpass observations and the SG60 vouchers / QR-code friction. Lara then surfaced her own Grab work — the Voice Assistant / AI accessibility product (80k local voice samples, DPM Gan Kim Yong reportedly interested) and the NCSS-proposed-but-abandoned "GrabCare" on-demand companion-matchmaking pitch. Zheng Wei reframed the space in design-thinking terms: pre-arranged elderly care is already solved through community; on-demand is the actual gap — the story arc for the sprint.

Lara then prompted a family-story round. This is where companionship emerged — not through analytical narrowing but by noticing the invariant across five very different family situations.

- **Shobhit**: his in-laws (early 70s, from Bangkok) live with him and his wife to help raise the kids. No mobility issues; socially isolated because they don't know anyone here. His mother-in-law is trying to build a network through condo gardening clubs.
- **Aditi**: her grandparents (in their 80s). The specific observation that losing a life partner creates a form of loneliness that children cannot fill in — this is her first-person family story, not a general framing.
- **Abhishek**: his parents and in-laws are in India; visit Singapore once or twice a year; he and his wife call every other day. Even with intact friend circles at home, their days lack purpose — "get up, take care of the house, watch TV." Not "lonely despite social circles" so much as under-stimulated / purposeless.
- **Lara**: her own elderly parents just relocated from Hungary / Eastern Europe to Singapore — "they arrived last week." She's actively looking for how to help them build community here.
- **Zheng Wei**: did not share a personal family story. His contribution is the HCAH context, ad-hoc research statistics, and the design-thinking framing.

Aditi named the pattern out loud: *"even though all of our circumstances are different, it all relates back to companionship."*

## The convergence was negotiated

Two alternative frames were seriously in play before we landed. Shobhit pitched **mobility safety** — his wife's grandma nearly hit by a car two days earlier, another relative fractured an arm falling on a public bus a week before that. Lara pitched **digital / AI literacy** as a natural extension of her Grab thread. We heard both and still landed on companionship because it was the only frame every family story pointed at. Worth remembering: the frame is a choice, not a deduction — and mobility safety and digital exclusion are still real problems we chose not to take on.

## Break, then the HMW exercise

After a five-minute break, Zheng Wei returned with numbers from his research assistant — around 400–450k single (never-married, widowed, or divorced) elderly in Singapore. Shobhit added the 20%-of-population-65+ rising to 24% by 2030. Shobhit then surfaced from Lara's whiteboard screenshots that the actual May 26 cohort deliverables are **an empathy map and an HMW statement**. That set the agenda for the rest of the session.

We ran the WHO / DO / OUTCOME exercise on a shared Google Slide (the Zoom whiteboard wasn't editable by external participants). Each person filled in their own row silently for ~3–4 minutes, then walked through it. Six rows in total — Zheng Wei wrote two, and one row was a collaborative edit that the group attributed to Aditi by default because several people were typing at once. Full verbatim record: [[hmw-2026-05-19-individual-exercise]].

The synthesis, honestly:

- **DO — "experience genuine daily connection."** The phrase appears in Zheng Wei's first row. When Aditi endorsed it verbally, she credited Shobhit ("I think Shobhit also wrote that"); it's plausible both wrote similar phrasing.
- **OUTCOME — Shobhit's synthesis.** He proposed mixing his own "quality of aging" language with Lara's "Singapore brimming with elderly communities" phrasing.
- **WHO** — deliberately broad, "elderly in Singapore," with a provisional 65+ stake. Abhishek pushed for 70+ (arguing many Singaporeans work past 65 so a companionship product bites harder at older ages). We deferred that to sizing data.

## Aditi's healthcare-burden reframe

The single sharpest strategic move of the meeting was Aditi's pushback on Shobhit's "increase life expectancy" as an outcome. Her point: from a government or investor lens, longer life in an aging society *is itself the pressure*. The right outcome framing is reducing healthcare burden and easing sandwich-generation caregiving load — not "live longer." Lara's chat message reinforced it: reframe from "how long" to "how well." That reframe is now the load-bearing strategic move in [[hmw-current]].

## AI reserved for the solution phase

Lara floated "AI companion" as a possible solution direction. Aditi held the line — AI belongs in the solution phase, not the HMW. Group agreed. This call held up under later field evidence (see [[../evidence/interview-round-1-findings]]) — the "cannot be AI" finding from a solution provider two days later would have made the HMW's implicit assumptions harder to unwind if we'd baked AI in.

## What we learned

Family stories are a better narrowing instrument than desk research when the team's own lives already touch the problem — five different family situations, same underlying word. The convergence was negotiated, not spontaneous; two live alternatives had real advocates. And "increase life expectancy" is the wrong outcome framing at the strategic level — Aditi's healthcare-burden reframe holds.
