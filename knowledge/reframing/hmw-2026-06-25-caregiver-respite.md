ok so this is the actual reframe. The [[hmw-2026-06-15-ncss-reframe-signal]] I opened after the NCSS meeting held the tension without making a call — three weeks later, in the [[../journal/2026-06-25-solutioning]] session, the team agreed to move. This entry is the new working HMW.

## The new HMW

> **How might we offer on-demand respite to caregivers of the relatively healthy elderly in Singapore?**

That's Aditi's phrasing on the Padlet, adopted by the group on 2026-06-25. It supersedes the May 19 formulation for practical purposes — [[hmw-current]] still describes the May 19 synthesis for continuity, but this is what we're now designing against.

## What's different

Three things moved from the May 19 HMW.

**Caregivers, not elderly, are the primary WHO.** The May 19 question aimed at elderly people directly ("help elderly experience genuine daily connection"). This one aims at caregivers who *need help delivering care.* The elderly are still the ultimate beneficiary, but the buyer, the user, and the pain-point-holder are the caregivers. That is a different product, a different user research plan, and a different distribution story.

**"Respite" instead of "connection."** *Genuine daily connection* was aspirational and broad. *Respite* is concrete — sudden-and-unexpected coverage of a caregiving obligation the caregiver can't personally cover in the moment. Grandma's sick, caregiver has a meeting, someone trusted needs to accompany her to the clinic today. This is the wedge NCSS had identified over a year of research and hadn't executed on.

**"Relatively healthy elderly" narrows the WHO the beneficiary sits inside.** Not the frail/bedridden — those need clinical care, not respite. Not the fully independent — they don't need caregivers. The productive band is the middle: seniors who need supervision or accompaniment but are still ambulatory. Matches Aditi's "unhealthy 60s and healthy 80s" framing from the NCSS meeting.

## What stays from May 19

Not everything gets thrown out. The parts that carry:

- **Elderly in Singapore** is still the ultimate beneficiary — we haven't left the space, we've narrowed the wedge.
- **Consistency is still the constraint.** The NCSS IDI finding — seniors need months to adjust to a new helper and *repel* new faces at first contact — applies to caregiver-respite just as much as to companionship. Any answer here has to reckon with it. See [[consistency-as-design-constraint]] (proposed).
- **Aditi's healthcare-burden framing** as the strategic outcome still holds. Reducing caregiver burden and sandwich-generation strain — arguably even more directly served by a respite frame than a connection frame.
- **Companionship is not gone** — it's now one of the respite categories the marketplace can offer ("2 hours of companionship, playing mahjong at grandma's flat"), alongside clinic escort, food pickup, and household tasks. Absorbed rather than replaced.

## Why the reframe held

Two independent forces pushed us here.

First, the round 1 field evidence ([[../evidence/interview-round-1-findings]]) — signal 02 — put the AI-companion path in real doubt. If the highest-leverage delivery of "genuine daily connection" was some form of tech-mediated companion, and the tech companion has a documented half-life, then the May 19 HMW was pointing at a solution space with a ceiling.

Second, NCSS handed us a sharper problem statement ([[../landscape/ncss]]) with:
- A year of prior ideation behind it.
- An operational pilot to point at ([[../landscape/community-chaperone]]).
- A funder willing to certify supply.
- A known gap (2–3 week lead times on existing providers, no on-demand option).

Given that trade — a broader-but-harder-to-serve frame vs a narrower-but-actionable one — the team took the actionable one. This is a strategic choice, not a discovery.

## What this HMW does well

- **Actionable.** Every word points at something buildable. *On-demand* implies a matching layer. *Respite* implies specific task categories. *Caregivers* implies a user we can interview, market to, and price for.
- **Anchor-partner-shaped.** Vanguard already runs the pilot (Community Chaperone). NCSS is willing to certify. AIC has blessed the direction. There's a path from HMW to POC that doesn't require us to originate all the pieces.
- **Business-case-legible.** NCSS asked us for quantifiable impact metrics. Caregiver respite has clearer denominators than companionship — number of caregivers, hours of respite, cost per hour, willingness to pay.

## What this HMW doesn't do well

- **It privileges an already-visible sub-population.** Family caregivers are visible because they're already inside the system trying to buy help. The far harder set — pride-gated seniors, isolated seniors with no active caregiver — get less served by this framing. We're taking on the tractable half. Worth naming.
- **The consistency constraint still bites.** Marketplace supply that rotates is the same failure mode as AI companionship. Any respite match that shows up once and never returns will fail. This is unresolved.
- **"On-demand" is a design goal that partly contradicts what the elderly themselves want.** NCSS's own IDIs said seniors want *consistency* — the on-demand shape triggers rejection. We are betting we can build something that feels on-demand to the caregiver and consistent to the senior, which is a real design puzzle.
- **Ownership.** *"I would not spend our effort over the next three months building something that nobody would take it forward,"* Aditi's line. Still unresolved. See [[devils-advocate]] critique 11.

## Devil's advocate — carried forward

The eleven critiques in [[devils-advocate]] mostly still apply. The new HMW does not neutralize:

- **Critique 6** (AI companion half-life) — softened, since we're less committed to a tech-companion product than we were, but not eliminated. The blue-sky data-labeling → humanoid version ([[../strategy/respite-marketplace-concept]]) still puts robotic supply on the roadmap.
- **Critique 7** (deck is same as one year ago) — inherited from NCSS along with the HMW. If we adopt the frame, we inherit the graveyard risk.
- **Critique 8** (algorithm is not the moat) — still applies. Whatever moat we build is not in the matching software.
- **Critique 10** (latent demand, awareness gap) — arguably worse for the respite frame than the companionship frame. Caregivers who need respite but don't know it exists won't respond to marketing until they're already broken.
- **Critique 11** (ownership) — carries forward unchanged and unresolved.

Critique 12 is new from [[../journal/2026-07-07-vanguard-prep]] — partners don't have skin in the game, so their enthusiasm is not evidence.

## What confused me / open questions

The gig-worker-vs-employee question is the biggest one. NCSS said they can fund certification. Vanguard runs the pilot with a mix of salary and micro-jobbers. What does "own and manage the supply" mean here — and is any answer defensible for a not-yet-anchored organization? See the pptx and [[../strategy/marketplace-2x2]].

The demand geography question. Is this hyper-local (block level, walking distance), township-level, or city-wide? Different answers give very different products.

The name question. "Respite-giver" is not a role, it's a description. Nobody bought "Care Pilot" / "TemanLah" / "C-3PO." Need a name that sounds like a real thing.
