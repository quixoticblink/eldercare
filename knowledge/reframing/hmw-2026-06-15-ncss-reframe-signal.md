ok so this is not a new HMW. It is a *signal* that a reframe is coming — recorded per the reframing-folder convention so the record shows when the framing started to move, not just where it ended up. The current HMW ([[hmw-current]]) is unchanged pending a team-level decision.

## What triggered this

On 2026-06-15 we met NCSS ([[../landscape/ncss]], full context in [[../journal/2026-06-15]]). NCSS presented their own HMW, which they've been working on for over a year through the Social Changemaker Series:

> **How might we enable caregivers to conveniently access respite in a timely manner?**

The relevant thing they told us — Aditi said it out loud — is that this is *sharper* than ours. Our current framing is companionship / genuine daily connection for elderly people. Theirs is caregiver-side respite for a specific stuck moment (grandmother sick, caregiver has a meeting, someone trusted needed *today*). Same beneficiary population; different unit of the problem.

Aditi's exact concession on the call: *"Your problem statement is a lot more specific than ours, to be very honest. Ours is quite broad at this point… companionship, or reducing loneliness among elderly. This is a very, very big problem statement, while I think yours is a lot more focused."*

## The three plausible moves

Not a decision — just the option space, so the team can pick.

**1. Adopt NCSS's HMW verbatim.** We become the tech-and-business-case arm of an existing NCSS initiative. Pros: sharper focus, real partner, real supply-side asset (Vanguard's Community Chaperone pilot), and NCSS is willing to fund provider certification. Cons: our own family-story-driven framing gets fully subsumed; the "genuine daily connection" language we spent May 19 earning would be traded for someone else's problem statement.

**2. Nest ours inside theirs.** Caregiver respite is the wedge; companionship is one respite category ("2 hours of companionship" alongside clinic escort, food pickup, night respite). NCSS floated this themselves — *"that could be one part of the marketplace."* Pros: keeps our thinking, uses their infrastructure, honest about the actual delivery mechanism (a trusted human, on-demand, in-person). Cons: risks becoming a feature request inside a larger platform.

**3. Reframe from scratch.** Take the round 1 findings, the NCSS IDIs, and both HMWs to a whiteboard and write something new that neither team has landed on yet. Pros: intellectual honesty. Cons: expensive; runway is tight; we've been through this exercise once and it wasn't fast.

## What the field evidence says

Round 1 already put weight on the reframe. Signal 02 in [[../evidence/interview-round-1-findings]] — AI companionship has a half-life — undermined the tech-companion path. NCSS's own IDIs ([[../evidence/ncss-idi-findings]]) sharpen it further with the **consistency insight**: seniors need *months* to adjust to a new helper. That contradicts the "on-demand" instinct at the heart of *any* marketplace answer, and it's a constraint neither our HMW nor NCSS's has priced in yet.

So whichever move we make, the next HMW iteration needs to name that constraint. A caregiver-respite marketplace whose supply is strangers with no continuity will fail the same way the AI companion fails.

## What we owe the record

Any actual new HMW file should be written *after* the Vanguard site visit and the follow-on IDIs. This one is a placeholder — it holds the tension so future readers can see what was in play at the moment we started to move, not just what we eventually chose.

## Devil's advocate — from this same meeting

The critiques NCSS unintentionally handed us are cataloged as items 7–11 in [[devils-advocate]]. Two of them bear directly on the reframe:

- *The deck is the same as one year ago.* NCSS's own frustration was audible. Are we walking into a graveyard of prior ideation?
- *Ownership is unresolved.* NCSS won't own it. Vanguard has interest but not confirmed capacity. Aditi's line held: *"I would not spend our effort over the next three months building something that nobody would take it forward."* Any reframe has to answer *who owns this in October,* not just *what is the question in June.*
