On May 19, 2026, the five of us each wrote our own How Might We statement using the WHO / DO / TO scaffold. This is the verbatim record of what each person wrote, preserved so we can see how the synthesized HMW (see [[hmw-current]]) drew from these — and so that future-us can look back and see how the framing started.

Format: WHO are we helping / WHAT do they need to do / WHAT outcome do we want.

The bolded cells are the ones we carried forward into the synthesis: ZW's first row's DO ("experience genuine daily connection") and LY's TO ("Singapore brimming with elderly communities..."). The WHO was generalized to "elderly in Singapore."

## What we noticed across the six attempts

A few patterns worth recording, because they may matter when we iterate.

The WHO clustered into two camps. Three of us (SS, AK, ZW) narrowed by living/family situation — "not working and no kids," "staying alone as a couple," "socially isolated." Two of us (AA, LY) kept it general. This split is going to come back when we segment for empathy maps; we already have an intuition that the problem isn't uniformly distributed.

The DO clustered around connection but with subtly different mechanisms. *Feel less lonely* is the most negation-shaped. *Feel connected with society* is broad and outward-facing. *Age with a close family member* is structural and specific. *Genuine daily connection* and *meaningful companionship* are about relationship quality. *Sense of belonging / assurance not left behind* is internal and emotional. The team picked the one that names quality and rhythm together.

The TO clustered into three scales: individual flourishing (age happily, feel alive, live healthy), systemic outcomes (reduce healthcare burden, increase life expectancy), and societal vision (Singapore brimming with communities). The synthesis went all the way to societal — deliberately ambitious.
