Leadership explicitly asked us to appoint a devil's advocate. This entry holds the strongest case AGAINST whatever framing we're currently leaning on — right now, [[hmw-current]].

The five seed critiques (drawn from [[hmw-current]]):

1. **Bottleneck is infrastructure, not companionship.** The real bottleneck for elderly community formation in Singapore is HDB layouts, void deck use, senior activity centers, and working-age children's time — none of which a software product can change. We'll build elegant software and nothing will change.

2. **"Daily" is the wrong frequency.** Real human connection runs on natural rhythms — weekly meals, monthly visits, seasonal celebrations. Forcing daily contact may degrade quality rather than enhance it.

3. **"Loneliness" is a Western frame.** In Asian family contexts, what elderly experience is grief about changing family structure (children no longer co-resident, filial duties eroding). Addressing it directly is treating a symptom of a value-shift that's already happened.

4. **Missed population.** Elderly with cognitive decline or dementia don't fit the HMW. "Experience genuine daily connection" works differently for them, or not at all in the way we mean it.

5. **TO is unfalsifiable.** "Brimming with communities" describes any outcome we'd see; no kill criterion. We'll declare success regardless.

## How this entry should grow

Someone on the team owns this role for one sprint. They pick one (or all) of the critiques above and argue it in earnest — not as a thought experiment but as if they actually believe it. The argument lives in this file. Status moves to `draft` when the arguments are written down. Moves to `solid` when the team has actually engaged with them and the HMW has been revised in response (or deliberately kept despite them, with documented reasoning).

The point isn't to win the argument. It's to make sure the HMW survives contact with its strongest opposition.
