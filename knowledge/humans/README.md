This folder holds two kinds of artifacts:

1. **Empathy maps** — synthesized views of a person or persona. Filename: `empathy-<name-or-segment>.md`.
2. **Conversation notes** — raw notes from a specific conversation. Filename: `YYYY-MM-DD-<name>.md`.

The flow: have a conversation → write the raw notes the same day → after two or three conversations with similar people, synthesize an empathy map.

This is the folder that produces *evidence of human centricity* for the rubric. If we want to be able to say "our solution is grounded in real conversations with real people," this folder is where those conversations live.

## Empathy map structure

When you create an empathy map, use these sections at minimum:

- **Says** — verbatim quotes that surprised you.
- **Thinks** — internal monologue you're inferring (flag uncertainty).
- **Does** — concrete behaviors and daily routines, not what they say they do.
- **Feels** — emotional landscape; highs, lows, triggers.
- **Pains** — frustrations, fears, blockers.
- **Gains** — what they value, what gives them meaning.
- **What surprised me** — the expectation that broke. This is the part that should drive HMW iteration.
- **Open questions** — what you still don't know about this person.

## Conversation notes structure

For raw notes from a single conversation:

- **Context** — why you talked, how you got introduced.
- **Notes (raw)** — bullets, verbatim where possible.
- **Quotes worth saving** — pull 3-5 best verbatim quotes.
- **My read** — honest interpretation, including what you might be projecting.
- **Follow-ups** — people they suggested, questions you'd ask next time.

## Capture rule for this folder

Write the conversation notes within 24 hours, while the language is still fresh. Don't try to write the empathy map from a single conversation — wait until you have at least two or three with similar people. The empathy map is a *synthesis*, not a transcript.
