## Context

An elderly woman who lives with her daughters and extended family in a multi-generational household. She cooks daily and helps care for great-grandchildren. Does not own a mobile phone — landline only.

## Notes (raw)

- Day: wakes, eats breakfast, thinks about what to cook, cooks lunch — "one pot of curry / dal" with rice and chapati. Watches the news on TV; dosai, idli; radio and TV "every hour." Babysits the great-grandchildren. In her own words: "scold the children I live with, but eat alone."
- Who she interacts with: "daughters who live with me, mainly family who live with me. No one outside."
- What loneliness looks like: "felt lonely once spouse passed away. Did not show emotion and only scold the children, negative thinking — connect one thing to another and think of bad scenarios. Very high expectations from my kids."
- What stops people admitting it: for her, nothing — "I will ask help from my caregivers freely and feel comfortable."
- Signs of needing connection (asked as a child-of-elderly question, answered about herself): "mother is very sharp and will ask me for help. No need to look for signs."
- A skill she hasn't had the chance to share: "I teach cooking to grand children and makes me feel more connected."
- On a younger person or neighbour spending time with her regularly: "I would want my children to look after me, but if really needed I can take help from others, but it wont feel natural."

## Quotes worth saving

- "Scold the children I live with, but eat alone."
- "I teach cooking to grand children and makes me feel more connected."
- Help from outside "wont feel natural."
- After her spouse passed: "did not show emotion and only scold the children, negative thinking."

## My read

She lives inside a full house and is still lonely — "eat alone" is the line that sticks. The connection that works for her runs through teaching: being the one who gives (cooking knowledge) rather than the one who is looked after. Her "wont feel natural" line is the charity problem stated plainly — help from a non-family member registers as charity, not connection. And her description of grief after spousal loss is worth noting: it shows up as irritability and catastrophizing, not as visible sadness. A solution scanning for "looks sad" would miss her entirely.

## Follow-ups

The "eat alone in a full house" detail — worth probing. Whether the teaching role could be designed for deliberately. The spousal-loss grief pattern — does it generalise.
