ok so this map synthesizes the three round 1 conversations where elderly people spoke for themselves — not caregivers, caretakers, or founders speaking about seniors. That's n=3, convenience-sampled, and they are not one person: one has engineered a coping day around an Active Ageing Centre, one has a full household and still eats alone, one has done "everything right" on paper and still hits empty-calendar days. Treat this as a first-pass segment map for [[hmw-current]], not a statistically representative portrait of elderly in Singapore.

## Says

Verbatim from the three conversations — attributed so we know whose voice we're hearing.

From [[2026-05-25-elderly-resident-rc-centre]]:

- "Loneliness is when I wake up without any activities planned for the day."
- "I tell myself to take it as a rest day but I find myself feeling very sad."
- "What else can I do when I have already tried?"
- "It's part of life. Children only come visit once in a while. Nothing more to be done."
- "neighbours — have known them for decades."

From [[2026-05-25-elderly-gentleman-toa-payoh-aac]]:

- "Not talking to anyone all day is biggest worry."
- "Doesn't like being at home."
- Connection through games: "banter / jokes / conversation with fellow players."

From [[2026-05-24-elderly-woman-living-with-family]]:

- "Scold the children I live with, but eat alone."
- "I teach cooking to grand children and makes me feel more connected."
- "I would want my children to look after me, but if really needed I can take help from others, but it wont feel natural."
- After spouse passed: "did not show emotion and only scold the children, negative thinking."
- "I will ask help from my caregivers freely and feel comfortable."

## Thinks

Inferred — flagging uncertainty throughout.

The RC-centre resident may think they've already done their part — join activities, keep neighbours, accept that children visit rarely — and that asking for more would be futile or ungrateful. *I'm less sure whether "nothing more to be done" is acceptance or defeat; it reads like both.*

The Toa Payoh gentleman may think silence at the centre is the real danger signal — not necessarily for others, but as his own worst case. He may not label himself lonely because his day has shape; he may think people who sit without talking are the ones in trouble, even if he can't tell from the outside.

The woman in the full house may think family proximity ought to be enough, and that needing outsiders would admit something shameful — hence help that "wont feel natural." She may think grief after her spouse should not be shown directly; irritability and catastrophizing may be how she permits herself to feel loss without naming sadness. *Projection risk: we're reading "scold" as masked grief — she didn't say that word.*

All three may think children are the legitimate source of care and presence — even when children are unavailable or only visit occasionally. Non-family help may register as charity or substitution, not connection.

## Does

Concrete behaviors from notes and observation — not aspirations.

**RC-centre resident:** Wakes 8am, coffee, market at 9, walk at 10, lunch 1pm, nap 3pm, Resident Committee Centre at 5pm, dinner 7pm. Social world is decades-old neighbours. Joins community activities when available; still has days with nothing planned.

**Toa Payoh gentleman (~70):** Out of the house ~4–5 hours daily — coffee shop / hawker, Active Ageing Centre perimeter even when not joining an activity. Attends games when offered; values banter with fellow players. Weekends: meals with son and daughter-in-law when they visit. Tries to talk to people who sit silent at the centre. Breakfast and dinner with wife.

**Woman with family:** Cooks daily (curry/dal, rice, chapati); watches news; dosai, idli; radio and TV "every hour." Babysits great-grandchildren. Teaches grandchildren cooking. Interacts "mainly family who live with me. No one outside." Landline only — no mobile. Eats alone despite shared household.

## Feels

**Sadness on empty days** — the RC-centre resident names it directly when the calendar is blank, even after reframing the day as a "rest day."

**Worry about silence** — the Toa Payoh gentleman names not talking to anyone all day as his biggest personal fear; he may feel comparatively okay when his day has ambient social presence.

**Loneliness masked or routed through irritability** — the woman describes post-spousal loneliness as not showing emotion, scolding children, negative thinking linking bad scenarios. *We didn't hear her use the word lonely in the present tense — only about the period after her spouse passed.*

**Connection when giving, not only receiving** — teaching cooking to grandchildren "makes me feel more connected." That may be a high point in an otherwise inward-facing routine.

**Resignation** — "part of life," "nothing more to be done," "what else can I do when I have already tried" — a flat emotional register that may sit alongside sadness rather than replacing it.

## Pains

Empty calendar days after doing what they're told to do — activities, neighbours, structure — and still waking up with nothing planned (RC-centre resident).

Presence without felt connection — full house, meals alone, family nearby but not the kind of company that registers (woman with family).

Children as the wanted caregiver who doesn't show up often enough — visits "once in a while"; desire for children to look after them, not substitutes (all three, different wording).

Non-family help that feels unnatural or charity-shaped — help "wont feel natural" when family should be the ones (woman with family).

Language and pride as barriers to new ties — Mandarin-only speakers struggling with non-Mandarin volunteers; shyness reaching out to strangers (Toa Payoh gentleman, observed about others and named as a barrier).

Infrastructure that doesn't guarantee participation — same AAC, same void deck; some people banter over games, others sit all day without talking (Toa Payoh gentleman).

Technology gap — no mobile, landline only (woman with family); we don't know if the other two are smartphone-comfortable from these notes.

## Gains

A day with a repeatable shape — market, walk, RC, coffee shop loop, AAC orbit — seems to protect against the empty-calendar feeling when it works.

Low-stakes, recurring third places — coffee shop, hawker, centre perimeter — where you see people even without a formal programme.

Activities that stimulate the mind and carry banter — games at the AAC, not just attendance.

Long-horizon neighbour relationships — decades of familiarity, not stranger matching.

Role and contribution — teaching cooking to grandchildren; being sharp enough to ask for help on your own terms.

Family meals on weekends or shared breakfast/dinner with spouse when the rhythm includes it.

## What surprised me

Loneliness defined as an **empty calendar**, not missing people per se — from someone who already has neighbours, activities, and a structured routine. That breaks the assumption that joining programmes fixes loneliness.

**Full house, eat alone** — household density is not a proxy for connection. Any solution that equates "lives with family" with "supported" will miss this person.

**The positive control in the same city** — Toa Payoh gentleman engineered 4–5 hours outside the flat and is doing fine, while others at the same centre sit silent. Same infrastructure, different outcome — agency and familiarity may matter more than another service on the menu.

**Grief that looks like scolding** — post-spousal loss described as irritability and catastrophizing, not visible sadness. Screening for "looks lonely" would miss her.

**"Nothing more to be done" from the engaged elderly** — the person most aligned with official advice (structure, RC, neighbours) is also the most resigned. Interventions that assume they haven't tried yet may insult the people who have.

## Open questions

How often does each pattern appear — empty-calendar days vs. eat-alone-in-full-house vs. self-built AAC orbit? Three interviews can't answer that.

What do the silent sitters at the AAC experience — are they lonely, content, or pride-gated? The Toa Payoh gentleman explicitly doesn't know.

What would the RC-centre resident accept as a "smallest meaningful change" — they answered NA; we didn't probe why or what they'd reject.

Can non-family connection ever feel natural — or is family replacement a category error and the design target has to be contribution, rhythm, and roles instead?

Do Mandarin-only and pride barriers block the same people as empty-calendar loneliness, or different subsets?

Where do elderly living genuinely alone (no household, no engineered AAC day) sit — we don't have a round 1 interview for that slice yet.

Does the woman still feel lonely now, or only in the period after her spouse passed — we didn't get a clear present-tense loneliness statement from her.
