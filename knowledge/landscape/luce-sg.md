Luce is a Singapore home-services aggregator that positions itself as *"the #1 Best Home Services Company in Singapore"* and has a genuinely operating **elderly-care and medical-escort service** that overlaps closely with what our HMW ([[../reframing/hmw-2026-07-08-post-vanguard]]) points at. This is not a partner — this is the closest live commercial competitor we've found to the caregiver-respite marketplace we're sketching. Worth understanding in detail because any pitch has to answer *"why not Luce?"*

Content below is from Luce's own service pages ([[#urls]]) and search-result summaries; the site itself is client-rendered so direct scraping returned empty shells. Where pricing is quoted with a "reference from Carousell listing" caveat, treat it as directional-not-verified.

## What Luce actually offers

Luce runs a **multi-vertical home-services platform** — home massage, home cleaning, and (relevant to us) **elderly care** and **medical escort**. The company's brand doesn't lead with eldercare; it's one line of business among several. That already tells you something about their unit economics and their conception of the space.

**Elderly care line:**
- Part-time caregivers for home visits.
- Booking framed as *"can start as early as same-day, depending on schedule."*
- Positioned around ongoing home caregiving — not just one-off tasks.

**Medical escort line:**
- Escort to and from medical appointments — the closest analog to what [[met-medical-escort-transport]] does with government subsidy.
- Same caregiver pool as the elderly-care service, per how the pages are structured.
- Explicitly targeted at the MET-gap that Vanguard's Community Chaperone was set up to plug (see [[community-chaperone]]).

## The supply side — AIC-accredited caregivers

The most important operational fact about Luce: **their caregivers are AIC-accredited.** Trained through AIC-approved providers, certified in first aid, CPR, and AED.

This matters for two reasons:

1. **The certification moat we've been discussing exists — and it's already been done by a commercial competitor.** [[../strategy/marketplace-2x2]] and [[../reframing/devils-advocate]] critique 8 both argue that certification-plus-trust is the actual moat in caregiver marketplaces (not matching software). Luce has already built that moat inside their platform. Any pitch that treats "we'll get AIC accreditation" as a differentiator is behind by a step.
2. **AIC accreditation is achievable outside Vanguard's channel.** This is a live proof point against the assumption that NCSS/AIC certification funding is uniquely available to us through the Vanguard partnership. It's clearly obtainable by an independent commercial operator too.

## Positioning and pricing

- **No upfront charges, no sign-up fees, no hidden costs** — explicitly stated on their marketing.
- **Same-day availability** for many bookings.
- **Pricing (directional, per third-party Carousell listing, not verified on Luce's own pages):** ad-hoc rate around $25/hr + 9% GST; repeat-booking rate around $20/hr + 9% GST.

The ad-hoc rate lines up almost exactly with what Vanguard charges for chaperone service ($25–35/hr per [[../evidence/vanguard-operational-data]]). Luce and Vanguard have converged on similar sticker price for adjacent services. What differs is the underlying cost structure and the delivery footprint.

## Subsidies context

Luce doesn't offer direct subsidies but flags that clients may be eligible for:
- **Home Caregiving Grant (HCG)** — $250 or $400 monthly for eligible seniors (means-tested).
- **Pioneer Generation Disability Assistance Scheme (PioneerDAS)** — $100 monthly for eligible seniors in the Pioneer Generation cohort.

Neither is Luce-specific — these are national schemes. But the fact that Luce advertises them alongside their pricing shows they understand the multi-payer stack ([[../strategy/respite-marketplace-concept]]) at the client-facing layer. Our pitch has to at least match this framing.

## What Luce doesn't do (as far as we can tell)

- **Crisis activation as a headline offering.** They can start same-day but they don't market around helper-runaway or spouse-hospitalisation triggers. The [[../reframing/hmw-2026-07-08-post-vanguard]] crisis-activation wedge is not obviously served by their positioning.
- **Care planning as a service.** No sign of the "caregiver planning" component that NCSS ([[ncss]]) named as the missing piece across the sector.
- **Institutional integration.** Not layered onto Vanguard's ICCP pilot or any other operator's platform — Luce operates as a direct-to-consumer play.
- **Corporate / employer benefit channel.** No sign of enterprise-adjacent framing.
- **Multi-payer stack integration.** Named the government subsidies but there's no visible plumbing to enterprise coverage, philanthropic funds, or corporate hotel partners (the components we sketched in [[../strategy/respite-marketplace-concept]]).

## Where Luce sits in the landscape

- **Direct commercial competitor** to the eldercare component of our concept. Not to the caregiver-respite crisis-activation component specifically.
- **Positioned adjacent to [[homeage]] and [[jagami]]** — the incumbents NCSS named at the 2026-06-15 meeting as having 2–3 week lead times. Luce's same-day availability is meaningfully faster than that baseline; whether they can actually deliver on it at scale is unknown.
- **Not on Vanguard's competitive radar** as far as the 2026-07-08 conversation showed. Vanguard named Homage but not Luce.
- **Multi-vertical company** — eldercare is one line of business among home massage, cleaning, and other verticals. That has implications for how deep their eldercare investment goes.

## Why this matters to our pitch

Three implications for [[../reframing/hmw-2026-07-08-post-vanguard]] and [[../strategy/respite-marketplace-concept]]:

**Differentiation must be crisper.** We can't lead with "AIC-certified caregivers, same-day availability" as our wedge — Luce already does both. Our differentiation has to be the *ICCP integration, the multi-payer stack, and the crisis-activation category* — the things Luce visibly doesn't do.

**Adds a *second* proof of viability.** Luce being live and marketed proves that a commercial eldercare marketplace *can* be built in Singapore. That undercuts critique 13 ("marketplace framing doesn't fit home care") in [[../reframing/devils-advocate]] — someone is already operating one. It also raises the question of *why haven't they scaled to reach the >50% unaware population NCSS documented?* The awareness gap is real regardless of whether a market exists to serve it.

**Sizing check.** If Luce is operating profitably in this space (unknown but implied by their marketing), that puts a floor on the addressable market. Whether that floor is compatible with the $425M SAM Grab modelled ([[../evidence/grab-eldercare-market-sizing]]) is an open question — Luce may be capturing 0.1% of it, or 5% of it, and either would be consistent with what we know.

## What confused us / open questions

- **Actual booking volume and revenue.** Nothing published. Would need to be estimated from market signals or requested if a partnership conversation ever happens.
- **Consistency handling.** How does Luce handle the [[../strategy/consistency-as-design-constraint]] finding? Do they assign a specific caregiver to a client for repeat bookings, or do they rotate? Not visible from public marketing.
- **Retention.** Do clients book repeatedly, or are most Luce eldercare bookings one-off? Retention profile shapes the unit economics but isn't public.
- **Relationship with AIC.** Formal, informal, or arms-length? Being "AIC-accredited" doesn't necessarily mean an active partnership.
- **Language and dialect coverage.** The [[../evidence/interview-round-1-findings]] language-barrier finding and the [[../evidence/marsiling-aac-grab-interviews]] Mandarin-only observation both matter; Luce's marketing is English-only.
