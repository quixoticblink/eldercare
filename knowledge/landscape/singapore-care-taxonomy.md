Singapore's eldercare sector runs on five distinct care types, and Vanguard's Jason walked us through the taxonomy in detail at the 2026-07-08 meeting. It's worth writing down because most of the confusion around "who does what for the elderly" collapses once the five categories are clear. Any pitch or business case has to name which categories the product touches.

## The five types

### Active Ageing Centres (AACs)

- **Target:** Well, robust seniors living in the community.
- **Nature:** Social, easy access, upstream preventive model.
- **Cost to senior:** Minimal — small admission fees at most.
- **Key role:** Delay frailty; keep seniors physically and cognitively active; outreach to isolated seniors.
- **National footprint:** ~240 AACs across Singapore. One AAC typically serves 1,500–4,000 seniors (~3,000 as a working figure).
- **Reach:** Despite the large well/robust population, AACs are only touching about **15–25%** of seniors in their catchment area. Capacity constraints and resourcing issues; also just difficult to get seniors to come out. The worry is about those who remain unreached, not those who show up.

### Senior Care Centres (SCCs)

- **Target:** Seniors with mobility issues, dementia, or rehab needs.
- **Nature:** Formalised, paid service; community-based (not institutional).
- **Model:** Day model — seniors attend in the morning, return home by 4–6pm.
- **Services:** Dementia daycare, maintenance care, community rehab (physiotherapy and occupational therapy).
- **Cost to senior:** Subsidised — some clients pay as little as $5/day.
- **Where crisis-respite fits:** SCC can absorb sudden-crisis activations that nursing homes can't handle (nursing homes have license constraints on short-term admissions). This makes SCC the operational flex layer for crisis respite.

### Assisted Living

- **Target:** Seniors who downsize (e.g., sell landed property) wanting peace of mind as they age.
- **Nature:** HDB flat ownership with wraparound care services layered on progressively as needs grow.
- **Key differentiator:** Residents *own* their unit. Care is added over time — custodial, dementia support, etc.
- **Model:** Currently a pilot in collaboration with HDB and MND. Vanguard's active site is Vivado; next is Bedok.
- **Why it matters:** This is the middle-ground model between independent home living and full institutional nursing — closest to what most seniors say they want when asked *"where do you want to grow older?"*

### Home Care Services

- **Target:** Seniors living at home who need care delivered to them.
- **Nature:** Staff visit homes to assist with feeding, toileting, custodial care.
- **Tech component:** Floor/movement sensors installed to detect falls or inactivity, triggering call-centre response.
- **The consistency question lives here.** The [[../evidence/ncss-idi-findings]] finding — seniors need months to adjust to a new helper — is most acute at home-care level, because the encounter is intimate and repeated.

### Nursing Homes

- **Target:** Seniors with high medical complexity — catheter, tracheostomy, significant nursing needs.
- **Nature:** Institutional; treated as *last resort*.
- **Model:** Not appropriate for seniors with only social or mild-care issues. Vanguard actively tries to *right-size* residents so only those who truly need it are admitted.
- **Vanguard's capacity:** 5 nursing homes, ~1,200 beds. Doubling planned over 5–7 years.
- **License constraint:** Nursing homes cannot flex to short-term respite — this is why SCCs absorb the crisis-activation load.

## Why this matters for our HMW

The [[../reframing/hmw-2026-07-08-post-vanguard]] targets caregivers of *relatively healthy elderly* — which lands squarely in the AAC + Home Care band, occasionally SCC, essentially never nursing home. The crisis-activation wedge specifically activates when a family reaches for SCC or Home Care outside their normal schedule.

Concretely, the respite marketplace ([[../strategy/respite-marketplace-concept]]) doesn't compete with nursing homes; it competes with (or complements) informal foreign-domestic-worker coverage, family-only caregiving, and the ad-hoc supply Vanguard already runs through its AACs.

## The distribution across the population

From Vanguard's whiteboard (see [[../evidence/vanguard-operational-data]]):

- Seniors 60+ in Singapore: ~1,000,000
- Robust (AAC-eligible): ~70% = ~700,000
- Non-robust: ~30% = ~300,000
- Frail (ADL needs): ~19%
- Severely frail: ~3%
- Middle-tier (needs some help but not fully dependent): 20–30% depending on sub-region

Our target population — caregivers of the relatively healthy elderly in crisis moments — sits primarily in the middle band. Not the robust majority (they don't need respite), not the severely frail (they need full institutional or intensive home care).

## Foreign domestic workers (FDW) in the mix

FDWs are the invisible fifth-and-a-half care layer in Singapore. They do custodial and household work, sometimes basic elder care, but not medical accompaniment, specialised training, or professional rehab. When an FDW runs away or resigns, the family is suddenly exposed to the full care burden with no infrastructure — this is one of the crisis triggers in the [[../reframing/hmw-2026-07-08-post-vanguard]] wedge.

Aditi asked this specifically at the Vanguard meeting. Vanguard's answer: FDW doesn't overlap with what Vanguard's respite services cover. The two supply sources are largely complementary, not competitive.

## What confused us / open questions

- **Where do fully dependent seniors fit** who aren't sick enough for nursing homes but need >3 ADLs of daily support? This is the ~44,000 population in [[../evidence/grab-eldercare-market-sizing]] — they seem to live in the seam between Home Care and Assisted Living, and it's not clear who serves them well.
- **How does an FDW-plus-Vanguard-supplement model work?** Many households already have an FDW; can respite services supplement rather than replace?
- **What happens at the SCC "day model" boundary?** If a senior needs SCC on a Sunday when it's closed, or on a weekday evening after 6pm, what's the crisis-activation pathway?
