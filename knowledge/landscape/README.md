What's already been tried in this space. One entry per company, initiative, product, or failed experiment.

For the rubric: *Quality of Thinking* rewards relevance to Singapore's tech ecosystem and clarity of strategic pathway. Knowing what's been tried, why it failed, and what's running — that's the substrate. Don't propose anything without checking this folder first.

## Current entries

- [[ncss]] — the National Council of Social Service. Statutory body above ~150 SSAs. Has their own caregiver-respite HMW held for over a year; approached us as potential platform partner after Grab and Gojek both declined. Won't own the platform themselves. Full context including funding they can bring and the ownership question they can't answer.
- [[vanguard]] — the MOH Holdings unit running the two active pilots most relevant to us. Potential anchoring organisation, pending site visit at Woodlands.
- [[community-chaperone]] — Vanguard's on-demand escort pilot. Manual matching, small scale, "well-received" but no formal survey. Closest existing thing to what a caregiver-respite marketplace would coordinate.
- [[snabbit]] — India hyperlocal home-help marketplace. Referenced by the team as a design archetype for hyper-local + lightly-vetted matching. Sits between marketplace 2×2 archetypes 3 and 4.
- [[urban-company]] — the canonical managed-marketplace archetype (2×2 archetype 4). Vets, trains, prices, standardises supply without employing them. The team's closest reference model for how a caregiver-respite platform might operate — with the "bleeding money at thin demand" caution Aditi named.
- [[aic]] — Agency for Integrated Care. Sector layer between MOH and operators. Holds the demand data (MET waitlist, PHV, sub-region breakdowns) that Vanguard doesn't have. Next partnership meeting after Vanguard.
- [[iccp-model]] — MOH's 85-sub-region operating framework. The context our pilot sits inside. Sub-region-native routing, aging-in-place strategy, operator-to-sub-region assignments.
- [[singapore-care-taxonomy]] — the five care types (AAC / SCC / Assisted Living / Home Care / Nursing Home) walked through by Vanguard's Jason. Naming what a caregiver-respite marketplace does and doesn't touch.
- [[care-corner]] — SSA operator; runs the Toa Payoh 170 AAC where our round-1 field interviews happened. NCSS-validated. Round-1 site-visit photos captured.
- [[lee-ah-mooi-old-age-home]] — small private old-age home on Thomson Lane where our round-1 frontline-caretaker interview happened. Boundary case for the private long-tail of the nursing-home tier.
- [[luce-sg]] — **live commercial competitor.** Multi-vertical home-services platform with elderly-care and medical-escort lines. AIC-accredited caregivers, same-day booking, ~$25/hr (ad-hoc) / ~$20/hr (repeat). Closest live analog to what we're building. Any pitch has to answer "why not Luce?"
- [[suss-gif-2026]] — Geronpreneurship Innovation Festival at Suntec on 26 August 2026. Potential exhibition venue for the September deliverable; also market-intelligence territory. Early-bird exhibitor rate deadline 15 July.

## Capture rule

When you hear about a company, product, or initiative that touches eldercare, drop in a stub here with: what they do, when they launched, what's worked, what's failed, who runs it, and (if known) why. If the entry is for a postmortem, document the *specific reasons* it failed — generic "didn't get traction" is not useful.
