Vanguard sits under **MOH Holdings** (the corporate arm of Singapore's Ministry of Health) and is — as their team stated during the 2026-07-08 visit — *the only Ministry-owned entity in the home-care sector.* Every other major operator in the sector is charity-based. That structural uniqueness matters: Vanguard can push MOH-adjacent pilots faster than a charity, and can be pointed at whitespace problems the ministry wants de-risked before scaling to the sector.

The organisation is about ten years old, originally set up to address Singapore's nursing-home-bed shortage. It has since expanded into the full care continuum.

## The organisation, current picture

**Physical footprint:**

- 5 **nursing homes**, ~1,200 beds. Aiming to double capacity over the next 5–7 years.
- Multiple **senior care centres** (SCCs) and **active ageing centres** (AACs), with 3 new centres opening next year in the Marsiling Ridge area.
- **Home care services** with tech-enabled monitoring (fall/movement sensors triggering call-centre response).
- **Assisted living pilot** in collaboration with HDB and MND — currently at Vivado, next site Bedok. Residents own their HDB flat; Vanguard layers care services progressively as needs grow.

**Model:** Vanguard is aligned with MOH's [[iccp-model]] — the country is divided into 85 sub-regions and operators are assigned to specific ones rather than serving nationwide. **Pasir Ris is Vanguard's designated innovation sandbox.**

## The two pilots most relevant to our HMW

- **[[community-chaperone]]** — on-demand escort for daily-life tasks (clinic, haircut, meals, discharge). Started April–May 2025 when Vanguard noticed that the subsidised Medical Escort Transport (MET) programme covers medical trips but not social trips. Manual matching, small scale.
- **Night Respite** — one certified staff on standby for overnight coverage during caregiver crises.

Both pilots have real operational data now (see [[../evidence/vanguard-operational-data]] for the numbers Vanguard shared).

## The operational picture (from 2026-07-08 visit)

**Scale:** ~160 clients served daily across 4 senior-care centres. 20–30 chaperone appointments per month. Estimated 5–10% attach rate in Pasir Ris.

**Supply mix per centre:** 3–4 Vanguard-employed trained staff + 19–20 senior micro-jobbers. Not mass-recruiting; first formal micro-worker onboarding cycle starts July 2026.

**Pricing tension:**
- Vanguard pays micro-jobbers $10–12/hour (below the $20–22 gig-market comparator).
- Vanguard charges clients $25–35/hour.
- Cost basis per session including overhead is ~$26.
- Many clients can't pay $25–35 — Vanguard subsidises through an internal healthcare fund and philanthropic capital.

**Matching mechanics today:**
- Cases come in by phone.
- A nurse does manual phone triage to assess complexity.
- First session is always a Vanguard staff member (not a micro-worker), because early sessions surface unpredictable scenarios.
- Subsequent visits: manual roster/WhatsApp check to assign a micro-worker.
- 3-day advance notice is typical.
- Rejection rate: roughly 3–4 out of every 20 requests, due to supply constraints.

**Micro-worker profile:** Well/robust seniors in their early 60s — early retirees who are fit but no longer need the income. Motivated by contribution and structure, not wages. Recruited through the AACs where Vanguard already engages this demographic. Free training.

**Training tracks:**
- **External certification:** CPR (VCRF EAD) done at St. Luke's Hospital. Community Assistance and Conserve Bandwidth modules also externally certified.
- **In-house (no cert):** Mobility assistance (half-day), dementia basics, emergency management, SOPs, on-the-job training.
- **Tiered readiness:** Only micro-workers who complete the dementia module get deployed for dementia tasks. Simpler cases (chaperone) only need basic mobility training. Complex behavioural dementia is reserved for Vanguard's own trained staff.

## What Vanguard is willing to bring

- **A Pasir Ris pilot by year-end**, layered on top of the existing ICCP platform. This is the answer to the ownership question we've been asking since June 15.
- **Job-scope + training breakdown documented** (Jason committed to share).
- **Sub-region population breakdown** (Angeline committed to share).
- **Willingness to write on the existing ICCP framework** rather than needing us to build a parallel platform.
- **Data cooperation to the extent MOH permits** (aggregate demand data is restricted; Vanguard can share their own operational numbers freely).

## What Vanguard won't do

- **Own the platform commercially.** Angeline was clear this needs to sit somewhere else — a commercial entity, an NGO, or a sector-level operator. Vanguard is willing to be an *anchor tenant* of a Pasir Ris pilot, not the platform builder.
- **Formalise a preference / rating system for care staff** — MOH and AIC have policy-level hesitancy about this. Any UX we design has to work around it.
- **Fund a national rollout.** They can pilot Pasir Ris. Scaling to the other 84 sub-regions requires MOH funding, which requires the data-driven demand case they don't yet have.

## Vanguard's own frustrations (surfaced in the meeting)

- They *tried* to pitch a formal caregiver-respite programme at the ministry level and were not funded. Angeline: *"we tried to pitch a program towards caregiving respite, but wasn't successful."*
- They're turning down referrals from **St. Luke's Hospital** due to capacity constraints.
- MOH will not release aggregate demand data even when it exists, for concern that it will be misused out of context.
- **GrabTruck** and other gig-worker teams have declined to collaborate previously — Vanguard has been trying to connect Grab-side without success. (This is the thread Lara is now activating.)
- No lead agency will own centralised care-worker certification — Vanguard wants it, nobody owns it.
- Nursing homes cannot flex to short-term respite (license constraints); SCCs have to absorb.

## What confused us / open questions

- **The sizing extrapolation carries assumptions.** 15–30K bookings/month nationwide is 20–30/month × (national base / Pasir Ris base). Real number could be much lower (Pasir Ris might be an outlier) or much higher (their supply constraints suppress observed demand). Wants independent triangulation via AIC.
- **What does "pilot in Pasir Ris by year-end" mean concretely?** Team size, budget, service definitions, tech surface, KPIs — all unresolved. This needs a follow-up.
- **How does Vanguard's phone-triage-then-first-session-with-staff pattern scale?** It works at 20–30 appointments/month. Does it work at 200? At 2000?
- **What's the actual overlap between Vanguard's micro-worker pool and the AV-displaced-Grab-driver pool** Lara is now chasing? Different demographics, different motivations, different pay expectations.
