AIC — the Agency for Integrated Care — is the layer between MOH and the operators delivering care to Singaporean seniors. Set up as a private company but plays a statutory-board-like role for the Ministry: administers grants, drives policy implementation, works directly with operators on the ground. For our sprint they matter for one specific reason: **AIC holds the sector-level demand data we need and Vanguard doesn't have.** Talking to them is the next partnership meeting after Vanguard.

## Their role in the sector

- Administers **grants and funding flows** from MOH to operators like Vanguard.
- Manages **national referrals from healthcare institutions** — hospitals discharge cases, AIC routes them to the appropriate operator by sub-region.
- Under the new [[iccp-model]] (Integrated Community Care Programme), cases will flow more directly from healthcare institutions to sub-region providers rather than through AIC centrally — but AIC still holds the referral history and the waitlist data.
- Runs the **Population Health Visit (PHV)** framework in collaboration with operators — the ~3-page in-home survey capturing seniors' need for support, coping status, and social interaction quality. See [[../evidence/vanguard-operational-data]] for the loneliness stats derived from PHV.
- Cross-agency counterpart to [[ncss]] — AIC serves the *health* sector; NCSS serves the *social* sector. There's growing pressure for health-social integration and the two agencies increasingly coordinate.

## Why they matter to us

The Vanguard meeting on 2026-07-08 kept pointing at AIC as the holder of the data Vanguard doesn't have:

- **Medical Escort Transport (MET) waitlist data** — the true demand ceiling for accompanied medical trips.
- **National-level demand for chaperone-adjacent services** — beyond what Vanguard sees inside its own four centres.
- **Sub-region breakdowns** — the 85 ICCP zones with their demographic profiles.

Without AIC's numbers, our sizing extrapolation (15–30K bookings/month nationwide, per the [[community-chaperone]] whiteboard) is anecdotal. AIC either confirms it, corrects it, or reveals a much bigger latent number.

## What Vanguard said about AIC

Angeline and Jason's characterisation, from the 2026-07-08 visit:

- **AIC has ongoing informal discussions with Vanguard** about expediting services and immediate care activation for crisis situations.
- **AIC is data-restricted** on what it can share externally — aggregate national numbers get gate-kept because of concern about mis-use.
- **Rating / preference matching for care staff** has been discussed at MOH/AIC level and there's *hesitancy to move in that direction currently.* This is a policy constraint we have to design around.
- **Under ICCP, AIC's central-routing role gets partially disintermediated** — cases flow directly to sub-region providers. This is a signal that MOH wants to shorten the referral chain.

## What we'd want from AIC

If we get the meeting, priority questions:

- **MET waitlist data** — how many caregivers currently wait for medical escort, how long, and what fraction fail out to informal help.
- **PHV-derived caregiver-need data** — the ~3-page survey should have questions on caregiver stress and unmet needs. Aggregate results at national and sub-region level.
- **Referral funnel data** — how many discharge cases per month need home-care follow-up but don't find an operator with capacity in their sub-region.
- **Sub-region demographic profiles** — Pasir Ris is Vanguard's sandbox; where does it sit relative to the older estates (Ang Mo Kio, Toa Payoh) or newer/mixed ones?
- **The policy stance on preference matching** — is the hesitancy against rating specifically, or against digital matching more broadly? What would AIC be comfortable with?
- **Skin-in-the-game** — the [[../reframing/devils-advocate]] critique 12 question. If we build this, what commits AIC to routing referrals through it?

## Where it fits in the map

- **MOH** is upstream — sets policy, funds programmes, owns Vanguard.
- **AIC** is the sector layer — routes referrals, administers grants, drives implementation.
- **Vanguard, SSAs, and other operators** are downstream — deliver the services.
- **NCSS** is the parallel social-sector counterpart to AIC.

For our HMW ([[../reframing/hmw-2026-07-08-post-vanguard]]) — Vanguard operates the pilot, NCSS convenes the partnership, AIC provides the demand-legitimacy data that lets us pitch national scale.

## What confused us / open questions

- AIC's "private company with statutory role" status is unusual. What does that mean for how they contract, partner, and can invest in tech?
- Under ICCP disintermediation, does AIC's role *shrink* over time? Are we betting on a partner whose central function is being redistributed?
- The relationship between AIC and NCSS in a caregiver-respite pilot that straddles health and social — who leads?
